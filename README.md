## A little about me

My dad was an engineer and taught me to solder before I could ride a bike. Before I was ten, I'd somehow memorized large portions of the hex addresses in *Dragon Warrior Monsters*. I've always been curious about how things work and what else they might be capable of.

That curiosity eventually led me into networking, systems administration, and information security.

I've never had much of a budget for tinkering. My home lab is a collection of secondhand equipment, salvaged parts, and hardware other people no longer wanted. Working with limited resources has taught me to be patient, resourceful, and willing to learn whatever a problem requires.

I'm a fan of Matthew B. Crawford's *Shop Class as Soulcraft*, particularly its ideas about practical knowledge and craftsmanship. I enjoy repairing and maintaining technology, not just replacing it.

I've always believed computers should be tools that empower people and enrich their lives. Making technology more useful, accessible, and sustainable is a big part of why I enjoy this work.

## How I work

My background is primarily in systems engineering and troubleshooting. I enjoy investigating unfamiliar problems, understanding how the pieces fit together, and testing solutions on real hardware.

I've also embraced AI-assisted development, or what I sometimes call *vibe engineering*.

After some personal and health setbacks, these tools have helped me return to creative projects that had become difficult to pursue. They've been genuinely empowering.

I'm open about using AI in my development workflow. I use it to explore codebases, organize ideas, and assist with implementation. I still believe in understanding the problem, checking the results, documenting limitations, and testing what I build.

I value open source, respect for upstream developers, and giving credit where it's due. I'm always learning, and I'm happy to collaborate with people who share that approach.
